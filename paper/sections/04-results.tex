\subsection{The marginal cost of one class changes sign with its working set}\label{sec:r-sign}
% Finding \claim{A1}: -0.0353 +/- 0.0053 -> +0.1605 +/- 0.0067 -> +0.4383 +/- 0.0055
%   origin requests per added agentic request, same class, volumes, cache, corpus.
% Caveat in the text, not only in the caption: this is the 12->36 req/s marginal; the net
%   effect 0->36 is positive (+0.392 +/- 0.158) — "reduces origin work" is retracted (R2).
% Honeypot placement by windowed working set (C3), details in Appendix.
The marginal origin cost of the agentic class changes sign as its working set grows.
When the agentic class is raised from 12 to 36 req/s, each added request costs
$-0.0353 \pm 0.0053$ origin requests with a working set of 0.19$\times$ the cache,
$+0.1605 \pm 0.0067$ at 0.58$\times$, and $+0.4383 \pm 0.0055$ at 1.93$\times$
(\cref{fig:sign}).\claim{A1}
Class, volumes, cache and corpus are identical across the three configurations; only
the working set changes.\claim{A1}

In our testbed, a working set is the set of objects from which our load generator draws
the requests of a class, with sessions built as described in \cref{sec:method}.
We set the agentic scope to 0.02, 0.06 and 0.20 of the corpus, that is 1,017, 3,051 and
10,170 objects, against a measured cache capacity of 5,274 objects.\claim{A1}\claim{C2}
At each scope the agentic rate goes from 12 to 36 req/s, the human and exhaustive classes
stay at 55 and 28 req/s, working sets are mapped separately across classes, and each point
is the mean of 5 runs.\claim{A1}
The marginal cost is the change in origin req/s divided by the change in the configured
offered rate of the class (\cref{sec:method}).

Consecutive points are well separated (Welch $t = 22.99$ and $t = 32.13$).\claim{A1}
The negative value at the smallest scope lies well below zero ($t = -6.7$), so its sign
is not noise.\claim{A1}

This is a marginal cost between 12 and 36 req/s, not the net effect of the class.
At the smallest scope, the net effect of adding the agentic class from 0 to 36 req/s is
positive: $+0.392 \pm 0.158$ origin req/s.\claim{A1}
A negative marginal cost therefore does not mean that agentic traffic lowers total origin
work in this configuration.\claim{A1}

By working-set width alone, the smallest scope is closest to the agentic clients of our
public honeypot: it touches about 693 distinct objects in a 143 s
window, against a 90th percentile of 670 for honeypot agentic clients
(Appendix, \cref{fig:a-honeypot}).\claim{C3}

\Cref{sec:r-accounting} asks where this change of sign comes from, term by term.\claim{B2}
