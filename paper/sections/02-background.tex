\subsection{Background}\label{sec:background}
% Section E: the 15 Sep 2026 change is an announcement for new domains and ad pages only;
% never "default block". No confirmation that it took effect (VERIFICHE §7).
Operators and standards bodies increasingly treat automated traffic by its declared
purpose.
Since 1 July 2026, Cloudflare lets site owners manage AI traffic by three use cases:
Search; Agent, a fetcher that visits a site to complete a job, often with a person
waiting; and Training~\cite{cloudflare2026options}.
The same post announced new defaults for newly onboarded domains on pages that display
ads, which we did not find confirmed as in effect.
At the IETF, the Web Bot Auth working group specifies HTTP message signatures for
automated traffic, built on RFC~9421 and carried in the \texttt{Signature-Agent} and
\texttt{Signature-Input} headers~\cite{ietf-webbotauth}.
The AI Preferences working group defines a vocabulary for preferences on AI training, AI
use and search~\cite{ietf-aipref-vocab}.
On our honeypot, 3.83\% of requests carry Web Bot Auth signature headers, and none of them
comes from the declared agents; we observed the headers and did not verify the signatures
cryptographically.\claim{C6}
All these mechanisms act on who the traffic is and what it declares; this paper asks
what that tells an operator about the cost of the traffic.\claim{D1}

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:rw}
\paragraph{Automated traffic and web caches.}
Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2025} vary the share of AI traffic from 0\% to 100\% on Varnish,
Apache Traffic Server and Memcached, and measure the overall hit ratio and that of human
traffic.
Their workloads fix the overlap between human and AI content, at about 20\% in the
Wikimedia setup and 10\% in the synthetic trace, and do not vary it.
They show that scan-resistant eviction policies such as SIEVE, S3-FIFO and ARC keep the
hit ratio of human-like traffic high under AI interference, and propose a composable
hierarchy of differentiated tiers for human and AI traffic, with a shared tier for the
content that both request, instead of strict separation.
A Cloudflare post~\cite{cloudflare2026cache} reports initial experiments in which SIEVE or
S3-FIFO in place of LRU could let human traffic keep the same hit rate with or without AI
traffic, and expects a separate cache layer for AI traffic to be the best way forward in the
long term.
We agree on the exhaustive class: varied on its own volume, it costs about 0.98 origin
requests per added request in our testbed.\claim{A7}
We add four things.
First, we measure the marginal cost at the origin and compare it across three coexisting
classes, rather than the hit ratio.\claim{A2}
Second, for the agentic class the sign of that cost depends on the width of the class's
reachable set (\cref{sec:r-sign}), and by windowed working set the agentic clients of our
honeypot sit near the narrow end.\claim{A1}\claim{C3}
Third, the overlap that these workloads fix changes the measured cost of a class
(\cref{sec:r-overlap}).\claim{A3}
Fourth, in our testbed the miss ratio of the exhaustive class falls from 0.858 to 0.825
when the agentic class is added from 0 to 36 req/s under the separated mapping, so
isolating the agentic class in a
separate cache would remove a measured benefit.\claim{D8}
The separate layer that Cloudflare envisions is organised differently: it routes human and
AI traffic to distinct tiers and, for AI traffic, varies the handling by task and by the
latency the task tolerates, from caches with moderate response times for
retrieval-augmented generation to deep tiers near the origin, or delayed service, for
training crawls~\cite{cloudflare2026cache}.
Our evidence says only that, in our testbed, isolating the agentic class has a measured
cost; it does not say that their design is wrong.\claim{D8}

\paragraph{Capacity-marginal and load-marginal.}
Two different marginals are easy to conflate.
A \emph{capacity-marginal} asks how much a workload gains from more memory: utility-based
cache partitioning~\cite{qureshi2006ucp} and Cliffhanger~\cite{cidon2016cliffhanger} steer
cache space by the gradient of the hit or miss ratio with respect to the memory allocated
to a workload, and stack algorithms~\cite{mattson1970} and their approximations,
SHARDS~\cite{waldspurger2015shards} and Counter Stacks~\cite{wires2014counterstacks},
estimate the miss ratio as a function of cache size.
A \emph{load-marginal}, the quantity we measure, asks how much origin work one more
request of a class causes in the current state of a cache of fixed size, when the offered
rate of that class increases while the others stay fixed.\claim{A1}
The two are related through the cache state but answer different questions: a
capacity-marginal tells an operator where to put memory, a load-marginal what admitting
more of a class will cost.
FairRide~\cite{pu2016fairride} treats free-riding in a shared cache as a problem of
fairness and incentives; we treat the overlap between reachable sets as a bias in the
measured cost of a class.\claim{A3}
Admission control driven by feedback on load or latency is well established, for example
in Breakwater~\cite{cho2020breakwater}, DAGOR~\cite{zhou2018dagor},
Protego~\cite{cho2023protego} and TopFull~\cite{park2024topfull}.
Feedback control is not a contribution of this paper, and we do not propose a controller.

\paragraph{Working sets and characteristic time.}
The working set of a program~\cite{denning1968}, the objects referenced within a window,
is the concept behind our windowed working set; the variable we manipulate is the reachable
set, from which such windows are drawn.
The characteristic-time approximation of LRU is due to Fagin~\cite{fagin1977}, was
rediscovered and named by Che et al.~\cite{che2002}, and was formalised by Fricker et
al.~\cite{fricker2012}.
Our change of sign is consistent with this theory: fed after the fact with the access
distribution of our generator, the approximation reproduces the sign and the measured
values, while a simpler prediction we wrote in advance failed (\cref{sec:m-tc}).\claim{B7}\claim{B3}
We therefore do not claim a new law of caching; what we add is the measurement at the
origin that a class label does not determine the marginal cost, its size, the distinction
between marginal and net, the overlap confounder and the effect on human traffic through
load.\claim{A1}\claim{A9}\claim{A3}\claim{B5}

\paragraph{Delivery architectures for agents.}
SemDN~\cite{hua2026semdn} proposes a hierarchical edge infrastructure that indexes and
caches web content at the granularity of chunks for LLM agents.
We do not propose a delivery architecture, and we have not tested our findings on
SemDN.
We ask a different question: whether, inside a shared infrastructure, the cost of a class
of traffic can be treated as a stable property of that class.\claim{D1}
