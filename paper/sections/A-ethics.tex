\paragraph{Testbed.}
All load experiments ran on infrastructure that we operate.
The load generator sent requests only to our own cache and application, and no
experiment sent traffic to third-party systems.

\paragraph{Honeypot.}
The honeypot is a public measurement site that serves static chapters of public-domain
books.
Its \texttt{robots.txt} explicitly allows all crawlers, including AI training and
retrieval crawlers, which it lists by name, and the server applies no rate limiting: clients
are not steered, restricted or slowed down, and every client receives the same pages.
The site sets no cookies and runs no client-side scripts.
A public page on the site states what is recorded and why, and offers to exclude the
traffic of any crawler operator who asks.

\paragraph{Personal data.}
The web server records standard access-log fields, including the client IP address, which
the site's public page discloses; the address is kept to check that clients claiming to be
a known crawler come from that operator's published address ranges.
The server rotates the log daily and keeps at most 120 rotated files; the snapshot used in
this paper is kept by us and is not part of the released artifact.
Our analysis reads IP addresses only in memory, as a grouping key through a keyed hash
(HMAC) with a random key drawn anew at every run.
It writes no address, hash or log line, and only per-class aggregates leave it.
We do not identify, profile or contact individual visitors, and we do not publish the logs.

\paragraph{Corpus.}
The corpus consists of public-domain books from Project Gutenberg.
We do not redistribute the texts: the artifact identifies the books by their identifiers.

\paragraph{Review.}
This is independent research carried out without an institutional affiliation, so there
was no ethical review board that we could consult.
We followed the principles of the Menlo Report~\cite{dittrich2012menlo}: respect for
persons, beneficence, justice, and respect for law and public interest.
The measures above are how we applied them: disclosure and an opt-out for the operators
whose traffic we observe, no load on third-party systems, no release of personal data, and
transparency about what the site records.
