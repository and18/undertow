Effect of reachable-set overlap on measured origin work. Shared: the agentic reachable set is drawn with the same permutation as the human one, so its bases coincide with the human popular head (overlap 99.7%; the human generator never draws rank 0); 59.8% of human traffic falls on agentic bases. Separated: a different permutation reduces the overlap to 2.1% and that share of human traffic to 1.0%. Removing the overlap raises origin work by +0.926 req/s (t = 9.63) at 12 agentic req/s and +0.800 req/s (t = 10.14) at 36, and lowers the agentic hit ratio from 0.882 to 0.817 and from 0.973 to 0.955. Mean of 5 repetitions, all four points measured on 28 Sep 2026 with the exhaustive class in its own k6 scenario; whiskers ± 1 SE. The previous measurement, with a traversal counter shared by all classes (not drawn), gave +0.882 ± 0.183 req/s (t = 4.82) at 12 and +0.872 ± 0.064 req/s (t = 13.62) at 36. The overlap is a bias of the experimental characterisation of a class's cost, not a property of the class.
